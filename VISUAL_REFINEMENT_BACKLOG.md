# Visual refinements after Phase 3

Phase 2 introduces original, editable regional muscle anatomy and refines the shared NMJ schematic and existing skeletal-muscle detail. The following work is deferred to keep development focused on the diagnostic laboratory and the subsequent Patient-Level Evidence Atlas.

- **Heart anatomy:** Review the anterior cutaway, chamber orientation, coronary anatomy and conduction labels with a clinician. Preserve the distinction between a conduction schematic and myocardial pathology.
- **Immune cells:** Develop a consistent, restrained set of original cell illustrations. Depict only source-supported cell types and interactions; avoid suggesting a universal immune pathway in the N=38 analysis.
- **Tissue-level inflammatory detail:** Consider layered muscle and myocardial microanatomy with explicit separation of literature-supported injury, hypothetical mechanisms and observed aggregate findings. Do not portray generic damage as cohort histology.
- **Consistency across anatomical models:** Review proportions, orientation, terminology, line weights, magnification cues and leader placement together. Keep channel/receptor symbols distinct from antibody-binding symbols. Obtain clinical anatomy review before a public research release.
- **Advanced interactions:** Consider an optional motor-unit-to-endplate zoom, orbital detail, diaphragm contraction and a muscle-fascicle expansion. Keep explanatory motion optional, offer static equivalents and honor reduced-motion preferences.
- **Mobile detail:** Explore accessible enlarged views for small anatomical labels while retaining keyboard controls and a visible selection indicator independent of color.
- **Scientific scope:** Keep illustrations conceptual unless a cited primary source supports a claim. Continue to exclude raw unpublished patient data, diagnostic scores and simulated patient recordings.

The Phase 2 figures are simplified and not to scale. The regional body illustration represents anatomical distribution rather than a particular patient or a complete anatomical atlas.
